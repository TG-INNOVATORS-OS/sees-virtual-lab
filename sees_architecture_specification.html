2. Detailed Specifications of the Four Architecture Layers

Layer 1 — Vehicular Ambient Energy Sources Boundary: Physical Vehicle

Captures three naturally occurring ambient waste streams during operation: (1) Kinetic Inertia released during vehicle deceleration events, (2) Thermal Gradient ($\Delta T$) between hot exhaust surfaces (200°C–400°C) and passing ambient airflow (30°C), and (3) Mechanical Vibration induced by road surface roughness through the vehicle unsprung mass.

Layer 2 — Energy Conversion Transducers Domain: Mechanical-to-Electrical

Three specialized converters operate simultaneously: (1) Permanent Magnet Motor-Generators producing high-power charging bursts during braking, (2) Bismuth Telluride ($\text{Bi}_2\text{Te}_3$) Seebeck Modules generating continuous DC baseload current via electron diffusion, and (3) Bimodal Dampers pairing high-impedance piezoelectric cantilevers for micro-telemetry with electromagnetic dampers for suspension travel.

Layer 3 — MISO Power Management Controller (Proposed Novelty) Domain: Power Conditioning

The core proposed novelty of S.E.E.S. is the Multi-Input Single-Output (MISO) Diode-OR Combining Bus. The novelty lies in the combination: surge (kinetic), baseload (thermal) and vibration channels joined by one diode-OR bus into a shared supercapacitor buffer that feeds a decoupled auxiliary load. The diode-OR technique alone is standard:

  • Cross-Conduction Prevention: Ultra-low forward drop Schottky diodes (1N5819, $V_f \approx 0.22\text{V}$) isolate each generator channel. When the kinetic generator spikes to 4.58V during deceleration, diode D2 reverse-biases automatically, completely protecting the 1.70V Seebeck module from over-voltage destruction.
  • No Switching Electronics on the Bench Prototype: the bench uses only passive diodes, so there is no controller standby draw. A vehicle-scale version may add an optional boost/MPPT stage; its losses must be counted in E_overhead.
Layer 4 — Decoupled Storage Buffer & Auxiliary Loads Domain: Electrical Supply

Harvested electricity charges an autonomous secondary buffer (electrochemical supercapacitor bank or auxiliary 12V LiFePO4 battery):

  • Surge Absorption & Baseload Storage: The supercapacitor absorbs transient deceleration spikes and continuous thermoelectric baseload, maintaining stable $2.4\text{V to }12\text{V}$ bus potentials.
  • Decoupled Auxiliary Load Delivery: Powers auxiliary consumers (LED headlights, digital instrument cluster, cooling fans, and telemetry radios) with zero current drawn from the main vehicle traction battery, directly extending vehicle driving range.

4. Core Engineering Benefits of this Architecture

  1. Surge + Baseload Complementarity: Solves the intermittency problem of regenerative braking by combining it with the continuous thermal gradient of vehicle exhaust.
  2. Electrical Immunity: No single generator can back-feed or damage another generator under any driving condition.
  3. Autonomous Decoupling: Auxiliary loads run completely independent of main battery state of charge, improving EV battery longevity and safety.
